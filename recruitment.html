Recruitment

Most bad hires start with an unclear role, not a bad candidate.

We run hiring for companies of roughly 10–50 people: defining the role, checking the pay, sourcing, structured interviews, and an offer that fits your pay structure. Then a handoff to onboarding.

The deeper diagnosis

Hiring is a process. Most small companies run it as an event.

Each step is sensible on its own. Together they make hiring slow, and every role starts from scratch.

  1. Day 1

    The role opens. The posting is copied from the last one.

    Quick.
  2. Day 8

    Interviews go wherever calendars allow.

    Flexible.
  3. Day 20

    Each interviewer forms a view. None of them wrote it down.

    Normal.
  4. Day 30

    The decision happens in a chat thread.

    Fast, finally.
  5. Day 34

    The offer is set by what the candidate asked for, not by what the role pays. The next hire is already harder.

The cost isn’t one slow hire. It’s that none of it is repeatable.

The hiring process · what we run

  1. 01

    Role scorecard

    What the role must achieve in six and twelve months. Must-haves kept short. What isn’t required, written down too.

    Feeds 04 interviews · the posting

  2. 02

    Pay check

    The range checked against your level structure and the people already doing the work, before anything is posted.

    Feeds 06 offer · compression check

  3. 03

    Sourcing + screening

    Postings, direct outreach and referrals. Every applicant read against the scorecard. A timeline with an owner.

    Feeds 04 shortlist

  4. 04

    Structured interviews

    Each interviewer assesses a different outcome, with set questions and a written rating.

    Feeds 05 debrief

  5. 05

    Debrief + decision

    Ratings and evidence side by side. One decider, named up front.

    Feeds 06 offer

  6. 06

    Offer + handoff

    An offer inside the range. A start date. Onboarding started before day one.

    Feeds HR administration · onboarding

Role scorecard

Client Services Lead · L4

Written before the posting. Every interview maps to one of these outcomes.

Outcomes at six months

  1. Owns four accounts, each with a written renewal plan.
  2. Runs a pod of three to the delivery standard without the founder.
  3. Resolves client escalations in the first conversation.

Must have

  • Has owned client relationships end to end
  • Has led at least two people
  • Writes clearly under pressure

Not required

  • Agency experience in our exact niche
  • A specific degree
  • Years of experience as a number

Range

  • $104K–$118K, checked against the L4 band before posting

What changes

Operational outcomes. You can check each one.

  • The role is defined before it’s posted.

    Outcomes first, must-haves short, range checked.

    You’ll see it whenThe hiring manager can say what success looks like at six months.

  • Interviewers assess different things.

    Each stage covers one outcome, with set questions.

    You’ll see it whenNobody asks the same question twice.

  • Offers fit the pay structure.

    Every offer sits inside a range that was checked against the team.
