Manager coaching guide

A design lead needed to talk to a senior designer about missed handoffs. Four years on the team, good work, a real problem. The lead knew it mattered. They didn’t know how to open it, so they didn’t.

Context
28-person studio · first-time manager · no written escalation points
Layer
Manager decision support · Performance system
Built
Prep worksheet · opening script · response logic · HR escalation gate · recap template
47

Key findingdays of delay, because the manager did not know how to open the conversation.

01

Forty-seven days

The cost of not knowing how to start. Two more handoffs were missed while the conversation waited.

  1. Day 0 · Apr 2Miss 1Files arrive the morning of production.
  2. Day 3“I need to deal with this”Lead mentions it to the founder.
  3. Day 17 · Apr 19Miss 2Production loses a day.
  4. Day 21Drafts an openerDeletes it. “It sounded like a warning.”
  5. Day 37 · May 9Miss 3A client deadline moves.
  6. Day 40Asks for helpBrings it to Operationary.
  7. Day 4545-minute prepWorksheet completed together.
  8. Day 47 · May 19The conversation28 minutes. Standard agreed.
02

What they planned to say, and what it needed

The first version isn’t cruel. It’s vague, and vague feels safer. It isn’t.

✕ What the manager planned to sayDraft · day 21
“Everyone thinks1 your attitude2 has been negative3 lately.4”
  1. Hearsay. Invites “who said that?” The conversation is now about the team.
  2. Character. Nobody can change an attitude on instruction.
  3. A verdict. Nothing to respond to except disagreement.
  4. No dates. Nothing to review against later.
What the conversation actually needsFinal · day 45
“I want to talk about three specific handoffs1 that were missed, the impact,2 and the expectation going forward.3 Then I want your perspective.4”
  1. Bounded. Three dated events. Not a pattern of personality.
  2. Why it matters. Production and a client, not the lead’s feelings.
  3. The standard. What good looks like from here.
  4. Their turn. Said up front, so it isn’t an ambush.
03

The preparation worksheet

Completed before the meeting is booked. If a line can’t be filled in, the conversation isn’t ready.

Conversation prep · Senior designerComplete before booking

  1. FactsDates, not adjectives

    Apr 2, Apr 19, May 9 — final files delivered after the agreed time. Tracker timestamps attached.

    Ready

  2. Expected standardWhere it’s written

    Final files in the shared folder by 12pm the day before production. Studio handoff checklist, March.

    Ready

  3. ImpactOn work, clients, team

    Production lost a day twice. One client deadline moved by two days.

    Ready

  4. Employee perspectiveQuestions, not guesses

    “What’s getting in the way of the noon handoff?” “Is anything about the checklist not working?”

    Ready

  5. Support already providedWhat and when

    Checklist shared in March. No change to workload since. No other support offered yet.

    Ready

  6. Desired outcomeWhat “done” looks like

    Agreement on the standard. Any blocker named, with an owner. Not an apology.

    Ready

  7. Follow-upDate in the calendar

    Written recap within 24 hours. Check-in June 2. Review against the standard June 16.

    Ready

  8. HR escalation pointDecided in advance

    If health, leave, family or discrimination comes up: pause, don’t probe, call HR the same day. See section 06.

    Ready
04

The shape of the conversation

Thirty minutes. The longest block is the one where the manager talks least.

0–2 minOpen

The script. Then stop talking.

2–8Facts + impact

Three dates. What happened after each. No adjectives.

8–18Their perspective

Ask, then listen. Don’t rebut in the room. Write down what they say, in their words.

18–24Agree the standard

Restate it. Ask what would get in the way. Name any support.

24–30Close

Recap tomorrow. Check-in date. Thank them for the conversation.

05

If they say…

The part managers worry about most. Decided before the meeting, so nothing has to be improvised.

Response logic
If they sayWhat it might meanDoDon’t
“That’s not what happened.”Different facts, or a different view of the same factsAsk what they saw. Note it. Check the record after.Argue the timestamps in the room
“I’ve got too much on.”A real capacity problemSeparate it: agree the standard, then look at workload as its own question.Drop the standard to make it go away
“It’s because of the account team.”A process gap upstreamAcknowledge. Take it away to check. Keep this conversation on their handoffs.Debate someone who isn’t there
They go quiet, or get upset.Surprise. It’s the first time they’ve heard it plainlyPause. Offer to continue tomorrow. The follow-up date stays.Fill the silence by softening the facts
They mention health, family, leave or a disability.A protected issue may be involvedStop. Thank them. Say you’ll follow up. Call HR the same day.Ask questions about it, or offer a fix
06

The HR escalation gate

Managers own most conversations. These are the points where they don’t, written down so nobody has to guess.

Before

  • This is the third conversation on the same issue
  • A formal warning is being considered
  • You already know about a leave, medical or accommodation matter

→ Talk to HR before booking the meeting.

During

  • Health, family, leave or disability comes up
  • They raise discrimination, harassment or retaliation
  • The conversation turns into a resignation

→ Pause. Don’t probe. HR the same day.

After

  • The standard isn’t met by the review date
  • They dispute the recap in writing
  • You want to change their role or pay

→ HR reviews the file before the next step.

07

The recap

Sent within 24 hours. Short, factual, in the same words used in the room. It becomes the performance note.

To
Senior designer
From
Design lead
Subject
Recap of our conversation, May 19

Thanks for talking this through today. A short recap so we’re working from the same page:

  • We discussed the handoffs on Apr 2, Apr 19 and May 9, and the effect on production.
  • The standard: final files in the shared folder by 12pm the day before production.
  • You said late client changes are landing on the same afternoon. I’m taking that to the account team this week.
  • We’ll check in on June 2, and review the four weeks against the standard on June 16.

If anything here doesn’t match your recollection, reply and tell me. I’ll add it to the note.

08

What changed operationally

In this demonstration.

  • The conversation happened.

    Two days after prep, not another six weeks later.

  • The guide became standard.

    Every manager uses the same worksheet before a performance conversation.

  • Escalation is written down.

    Managers know where their decision ends and HR’s begins, before it matters.
