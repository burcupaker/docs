Work example 01 of 04 · Performance system

Performance documentation standard

An account director wanted to exit a project manager. HR asked what was on file. There were eight notes across five months. Most of them recorded how the manager felt, not what happened.

Context
34-person agency, one HR generalist, managers writing their own notes
Layer
Performance system · Manager decision support
Built
File audit · documentation standard · manager template · 45-minute manager session
5

Key findingmanager notes contained a conclusion but no observable example.

01

The file, audited

Every note, checked against three questions. Would it hold up if someone outside the company read it?

  • 2 partial
  • 1 usable

Five months of notes. One that could be relied on. The manager wasn’t careless — nobody had ever said what a note needed to contain.

02

The standard

Six elements. A note is complete when all six are there. Evidence and opinion are kept apart on purpose.

  1. Dated example

    A specific occurrence, with the date it happened.

    Passes“Mar 4: client revision sat 3 business days without a reply.”

    Fails“Recently.” “Lately.” “Again.”

  2. Observable behavior

    What someone could have seen or heard. Not what it meant.

    Passes“Status not posted before Monday stand-up.”

    Fails“Not owning the account.”

  3. Expected standard

    The bar, written so you could tell if it was met.

    Passes“Acknowledge client requests within 1 business day.”

    Fails“Be more proactive.”

  4. Employee response

    Their account, in their words. Recorded, not rebutted.

    Passes“Said two accounts made Fridays unrealistic.”

    Fails“Was defensive.”

  5. Support provided

    What the company did to help, and when.

    Passes“Priority order agreed Mar 20.”

    Fails“We’ve given a lot of support.”

  6. Next review date

    When this gets looked at again, and against what.

    Passes“Apr 17, against the standard above.”

    Fails“We’ll see how it goes.”

03

Opinion words, replaced

Managers kept this list open while writing. If a word on the left shows up, the note isn’t finished.

  • “Attitude”→What they said or did, and when
  • “Not a team player”→Which handoff, which meeting, what happened
  • “Communication issue”→Which message, sent when, expected when
  • “Always” / “never”→The dates you can actually list
  • “Lack of ownership”→The task, who owned it, what was missed
  • “Everyone feels”→What you observed yourself
04

One note, rewritten

N3 rebuilt with the manager, using their own calendar, email and the project tracker. Nothing was added that the manager couldn’t point to.

06

What changed operationally

In this demonstration.

  • The exit was paused, not cancelled.

    The decision moved to Apr 17, when it could be made on evidence — either way.

  • One template, every manager.

    Notes on similar issues now look the same, whoever writes them.

  • HR checks a sample.

    Five notes a quarter, against the six elements. Gaps go back to the manager, not into a file.
