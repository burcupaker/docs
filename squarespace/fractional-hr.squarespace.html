The deeper diagnosis

The company grew. The people infrastructure stayed informal.

Nobody chose to run HR this way. It happened one reasonable decision at a time.

  1. Employee 6

    A strong candidate asks for $9K more than the team makes. You say yes.

    Reasonable.
  2. Employee 14

    A manager gives a verbal warning and doesn’t write it down. It was a first conversation.

    Reasonable.
  3. Employee 22

    You hire in a second state. Payroll sets it up. Nobody reviews the rest.

    Reasonable.
  4. Employee 31

    A different manager documents everything, formally, from day one.

    Also reasonable.
  5. Employee 38 — now

    The $9K is your pay structure. The two managers are your termination standard. The second state is your compliance posture.

No single decision was wrong. Together, they became the policy — and nobody wrote it down.

Manager coaching guide

✕What the manager planned to say

“Everyone thinks your attitude has been negative lately.”

“Attitude” can’t be observed. “Everyone” turns it into a verdict and starts an argument about who said it.

What the conversation actually needs

“I want to talk about three specific handoffs that were missed, the impact, and the expectation going forward. Then I want your perspective.”

Specific. Bounded. Tells them what’s coming and that they get to respond.

The preparation tool · completed before the meeting is booked

  1. FactsApr 2, Apr 19, May 9: files handed off after the agreed time. Dates, not adjectives.
  2. Expected standardFinal files in the shared folder by 12pm the day before production.
  3. ImpactProduction lost a day twice. One client deadline moved.
  4. Their perspectiveAsk before concluding. What’s getting in the way?
  5. Support already givenChecklist shared in March. No change to workload since.
  6. Desired outcomeAgreement on the standard. Any blocker named and owned.
  7. Follow-upWritten recap within 24 hours. Check-in in two weeks.
  8. HR escalation pointDecided before the meeting, not during it →

Stop and bring in HR if…

  • They raise health, family, leave or disability
  • They raise discrimination, harassment or retaliation
  • This is the third conversation on the same issue
  • You are considering a formal warning

Yes → pause. Same-day call with HR.

No → hold the conversation within five business days.

What changes

Operational outcomes. You can check each one.

  • Managers stop improvising.

    Similar situations follow the same operating standard, whichever manager is handling them.

    You’ll see it whenTwo managers’ notes on the same kind of issue look the same.

  • Pay decisions connect.

    Offers, merit, promotions and hiring feed the same architecture and the same forecast.

    You’ll see it when“What does this offer do to the year?” gets answered in the same meeting.

  • Compliance has ownership.

    Leadership can see what is current, what is exposed and who owns the next action.

    You’ll see it whenEvery recurring check has a name, a piece of evidence and a date next to it.

    Sensitive questions still escalate when they should. Not because nobody else knows what to do.
