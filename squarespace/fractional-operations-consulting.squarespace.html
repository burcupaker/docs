If three of these sound familiar, the issue isn’t effort. It’s that the company is still running on conversations.

The deeper diagnosis

The company grew. The operating system stayed verbal.

At ten people, talking is the process. Past twenty, it stops scaling, and nobody notices the moment it happens.

  1. 8 people

    Everyone is in every conversation. Decisions happen in passing.

    It works.
  2. 15 people

    The first team lead. The founder still approves everything.

    It mostly works.
  3. 25 people

    Three tools for projects. Nobody’s sure which one is right.

    Workable.
  4. 35 people

    A key person leaves. Two processes leave with them.

    Painful.
  5. Now

    The founder is the router, the meeting is status updates and every process has one person who really knows it.

Nobody designed it this way. It just never got designed.

What Operationary does

Ownership, process and cadence. Written down, then run.

We sit between the leadership team and the day-to-day work. We build the parts of the operating system that tell people who owns what, how things get done and when decisions get made — then run them with you until they hold.

Turned into an operating model

The operating layer · what we build

  1. 01

    Ownership map

    Every recurring outcome and decision with one owner, a decider and who needs to know. Thresholds for what gets escalated.

    Feeds 03 agendas · 04 decisions · 06 roles

  2. 02

    Core processes

    The ten or so processes the business depends on, written down with an owner, a trigger and a definition of done.

    Feeds 05 systems · onboarding

  3. 03

    Operating cadence

    Weekly, monthly and quarterly meetings with fixed agendas and a named chair. Each one ends with owners and dates.

    Feeds 04 decision log · 05 scorecard

  4. 04

    Decision log

    What was decided, by whom, when and why. Checked before anything is reopened.

    Feeds 01 ownership · 03 reviews

  5. 05

    Scorecard + systems

    A short set of numbers the leadership team actually looks at, and one agreed source of truth for each.

    Feeds 03 weekly review

  6. 06

    Org + role design

    Roles defined by outcomes and decisions, not tasks. The structure for the next stage of growth.

    Feeds 01 ownership · hiring

  1. Weeks 1–3

    Map it

    We interview the leadership team, sit in the meetings and map who actually decides and does what today.

  2. Weeks 4–10

    Build it

    Ownership map, core processes and the cadence, built with the people who will use them. Tested in real weeks.

  3. Ongoing

    Run it, then hand it off

    We chair or support the cadence until it holds, then hand it to an internal owner.

What changes

Operational outcomes. You can check each one.

  • Decisions stay decided.

    The decision log is checked before anything is reopened.

    You’ll see it whenThe same topic doesn’t come back to the leadership meeting three weeks running.

  • Meetings end with owners.

    Each item leaves the room with a name and a date.

    You’ll see it whenAction items are written down before the meeting ends.

  • Processes survive a resignation.

    Core processes are written down, with an owner and a backup.

    You’ll see it whenSomeone new can run it from the document.

  • The founder stops being the router.

    Decisions below a written threshold get made without them.

    You’ll see it whenThe founder takes a week off and nothing waits for them.
