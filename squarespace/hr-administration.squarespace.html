The deeper diagnosis

The work isn’t hard. It’s recurring, and every piece has a deadline.

Small companies rarely get HR administration wrong on purpose. It slips one cycle at a time.

  1. Cycle 1

    A raise is approved in a meeting. Payroll finds out when the employee asks where it is.

    Fixed next cycle.
  2. Cycle 4

    A new hire starts. The benefits deadline goes on a sticky note.

    Caught in time.
  3. Cycle 9

    Someone leaves. Final pay is worked out from memory and a search.

    Probably right.
  4. Cycle 14

    An employee moves states. The HRIS is updated. Payroll isn’t.

    Found at year end.
  5. Cycle 20 — now

    Every run depends on one person remembering six kinds of change. When they’re out, nobody knows what’s pending.

Nothing here needs a strategy. It needs a calendar, a checklist and an owner.

What changes

Operational outcomes. You can check each one.

  • Changes reach payroll before cutoff.

    Every hire, raise, exit and move goes through one intake, matched to an approval.

    You’ll see it whenNobody asks where their raise went.
